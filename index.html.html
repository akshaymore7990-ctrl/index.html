Why this matters

Two failures, one broken feedback loop

Counterfeit medicines enter the supply chain because verification is hard. Harmful reactions go unrecorded because reporting is harder. Each failure hides the other.

~1 in 10

Medicines in low- and middle-income countries are estimated to be substandard or falsified

Counterfeits don't just fail to treat — they erode trust in every genuine pack on the shelf and put patients at direct risk.

SOURCE: WHO ESTIMATE, LMIC MARKETS
< 1%

Of adverse drug reactions are believed to reach formal pharmacovigilance systems

When reactions go unreported, dangerous batches stay in circulation longer, and regulators act on a fraction of the real signal.

SOURCE: PUBLISHED UNDER-REPORTING ESTIMATES
The platform

Four systems. One scan.

Everything runs from a smartphone camera at the pharmacy counter or the patient's home — no new hardware, no training manual.

Dynamic QR verification

Every pack carries a one-time, server-validated code. A genuine code verifies once for its patient; a cloned code exposes itself the moment it's scanned twice.

Anti-counterfeit

Computer-vision packaging checks

The camera compares fonts, print quality, holograms and layout against the manufacturer's reference pack — catching fakes that copy the code but not the box.

Anti-counterfeit

NLP-powered reaction reporting

Patients describe what happened in their own words, in their own language. The system structures it into a regulator-ready report — no forms, no medical vocabulary required.

Pharmacovigilance

Tamper-evident audit log

Every verification and every report is written to a blockchain-backed ledger. Manufacturers, regulators and auditors see the same record — and nobody can quietly edit it.

Compliance by design

Built inside India's regulatory frame, not around it

Health data is sensitive and the rules are specific. PharmaGuard AI treats compliance as an architecture decision made on day one — not a retrofit before launch.

DPDP Act 2023

Consent-first data handling

Patient reports are collected with explicit consent, minimised to what the report requires, and stored under India's Digital Personal Data Protection framework.

PvPI

A pipeline into national pharmacovigilance

Reports are structured to feed the Pharmacovigilance Programme of India, so a patient's one-tap report can become a national safety signal.

CDSCO

Regulator-legible records

Verification and incident logs are formatted for regulatory review, giving the Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation an evidence trail — not a data dump.

Schedule M

Manufacturer-side traceability

Batch-level verification data maps to good-manufacturing-practice requirements, helping manufacturers demonstrate control over what leaves their line.
